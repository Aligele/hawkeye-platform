Ward Voter Survey

Kenya · presidential election study
Read to the respondent: “Hello. My name is __________. I am doing a study on how people in this ward view the next presidential election. It takes about 15 minutes. Your answers are anonymous and confidential. We will not record your name and nobody will be able to tell what you personally said. At the end I will ask whether you are willing to be contacted for future polls. That is optional, and any phone number or email you give is kept separately from your answers. Taking part is voluntary, you may skip any question, and you may stop at any time. Do you agree to take part?”
Read each question exactly as written, in a neutral tone. Do not read out candidate names. Never record a name or ID number. A phone number or email is taken only in the optional last section, with the respondent's agreement.

Ward GPS fences

Enumerators can submit for a ward only while their phone is inside its fence. Every ward uses its real boundary by default (a ring around its centre where no boundary file exists). A fence you save here replaces the boundary with a circle; Reset to default restores it.

Questionnaires

Add the questions here and make them active. Enumerators then answer them after the interview details and screening. Close or delete a questionnaire when fieldwork is done; the built-in questions come back when none is active.

Paste many questions at once

Separate questions with a blank line. The first line is the question; each following line is an answer option. A question with no options becomes short text. Add (multi) or (multi 3) at the end of the question for multiple choice, and * for required. Start a line with # to name a section.

Word (.docx), text (.txt, .md), CSV or JSON. In Word and text files, put a blank line between questions and write the answers on the lines under each question, as in the paste box.
